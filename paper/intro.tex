\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Vision-language-action models (VLAs) have made large-scale pretraining the dominant recipe for robot
manipulation~\citep{rt2, openvla, pi0}. A pretrained backbone is adapted to a target task by
supervised fine-tuning on teleoperated demonstrations. The weakness of this recipe is the data it
imitates. Demonstrations interleave expert behavior with hesitation, detours, and failure, and their
quality varies even within a single trajectory~\citep{grrl, reinbot}; because an imitation objective
treats every transition as an equally valid target, the policy converges to the average of this
mixture. Additional data reproduces the same average more faithfully, filtering discards the scarce
experience the recipe depends on, and refining the policy through online interaction remains costly
and unsafe at this scale.

Can the policy instead learn more from the data it already has? Offline reinforcement learning (RL)
is designed for precisely this setting. Temporal-difference (TD) learning propagates recorded
outcomes through the dataset, stitching high-value segments of mediocre trajectories into behavior
that no single demonstration exhibits~\citep{levine2020offline, fu2020d4rl}. A VLA, however, is not
the policy for which this machinery was developed. Its backbone cannot be trained jointly with a
critic at practical cost, and its long action chunks~\citep{act, pi0} pose a dilemma for value
learning. One-step TD degrades over the long horizons these tasks involve, while chunk-level TD
(Q-chunking), which restores tractability by backing up over the whole chunk at once, inherits the
chunk's fixed open-loop commitment and becomes suboptimal exactly where reactivity
matters~\citep{qc}.

Existing adaptations of RL to VLAs stop at different points. Most retain the supervised objective and
let outcome signals reshape it~\citep{pistar06, arfm, arm, reinbot, nora15}, inheriting its ceiling.
Others learn a value but apply it around the policy rather than to it, filtering the
dataset~\citep{grrl} or selecting among sampled chunks at test time~\citep{vgps, deas}; either way the
value only chooses among options that already exist, and sampling-based selection in particular is
bounded by what the sampler proposes and biased toward overestimated candidates~\citep{emaq}. The
most direct attempts run actor-critic RL over chunks~\citep{corft}, but treat the chunk as a fixed,
indivisible action whose full length is executed in every state, even though a critic
scoring whole chunks of closed-loop demonstrations conflates skill with fortunate
outcomes~\citep{dqc}, and even though the appropriate commitment plainly differs between free space
and contact. The chunk, the very feature that makes VLAs practical, is what current offline RL
handles most poorly.

We propose an offline reinforcement fine-tuning method that improves the policy directly, by
optimizing over the joint space of what to do and how long to commit. Chunking is what makes the long horizon tractable, since backing up over
$h$ steps at once is the reason chunked TD remains effective in sparse, long-horizon
manipulation~\citep{qc}; valuing every prefix of the chunk preserves this benefit while restoring the
reactivity that a fixed commitment forfeits. Human motor control offers a natural precedent. Skilled
movement alternates between non-Markovian segments, executed ballistically from an internal plan
without consulting the current state, and reactive phases under feedback whose delays render even the
corrections intermittent~\citep{woodworth1899, todorov2002, gawthrop2011}. Concretely, a
causal-transformer critic is learned entirely offline on the VLA's frozen representations with
conservative in-sample targets~\citep{iql, deas}, and the policy is updated against it in both
dimensions of its output. The actions within the chunk are pulled toward what the value prefers, and
the commitment length becomes a state-dependent decision rather than a constant. This positions our method against each line of prior work.
Prefix-valued critics have appeared as RL algorithms in state-based settings~\citep{acsac, aqc},
whereas we turn the construction into a post-training instrument for a pretrained VLA; unlike
fixed-chunk actor-critic fine-tuning~\citep{corft}, optimization covers commitments as well as
actions; and unlike test-time selection~\citep{vgps, deas}, the policy itself is updated, with
nothing sampled and ranked at deployment.
Freezing the backbone constrains where knowledge resides, not the quality of the resulting behavior.
The support of the demonstrations contains better behavior than their average, which imitation blurs
and extraction recovers.

In summary, our main contributions are threefold:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We analyze why the prevailing mechanisms for applying a learned value to a VLA,
    sampling-based selection and fixed-length execution, cannot surpass the policy they start from,
    and locate the missing piece in direct policy optimization over both actions and
    commitments~\citep{emaq, dqc, park2024bottleneck}.
    \item We present a complete offline reinforcement fine-tuning pipeline in which a prefix-valued
    critic over frozen VLA representations drives direct policy improvement over both actions and
    commitment lengths; isolating its ingredients shows that in-sample targets and target networks
    are decisive, while low expectiles and unbounded value supports fail silently.
    \item Through experiments on VLA-scale manipulation~\citep{robocasa}, with every design choice
    isolated against its alternatives in controlled ablations, we show that the resulting policies
    surpass supervised fine-tuning using only the demonstrations it already had, without a single
    additional rollout.
\end{itemize}
Together, these findings establish offline value learning, applied where the value can genuinely act,
as a practical path to improving VLAs beyond the quality of their demonstrations.
