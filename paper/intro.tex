\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Vision-language-action models (VLAs) have made large-scale pretraining the dominant recipe for robot
manipulation~\citep{rt2, openvla, pi0}. The pretrained backbone provides priors strong enough that a
new task rarely needs to be learned from scratch; in practice, a VLA is deployed by fine-tuning it on
demonstrations of the target task.

The limitation of this recipe lies in the data it imitates. Teleoperated demonstrations interleave
expert behavior with hesitation, detours, and failure, and their quality varies even within a single
trajectory~\citep{grrl, reinbot}. Because an imitation objective treats every transition as an
equally valid target, additional data only reproduces the same average behavior more faithfully;
filtering discards the scarce experience the recipe depends on, and refining the policy through
online interaction remains costly and unsafe at this scale.

Can an optimal policy be extracted from suboptimal human data at VLA scale? Temporal-difference (TD)
learning promises precisely this, stitching high-value segments of mediocre trajectories into
behavior that no single demonstration exhibits~\citep{levine2020offline, fu2020d4rl}. A VLA, however,
is not the policy for which this machinery was designed: it predicts a fixed, long action
chunk~\citep{act, pi0}, it operates over long horizons where one-step TD degrades~\citep{qc}, and its
backbone cannot be trained jointly with a critic at practical cost.

Recent attempts fall short of this promise. Most reshape the imitation objective with outcome
signals~\citep{pistar06, arfm, arm, reinbot, nora15}, and thereby inherit both the stability and the
ceiling of supervised learning. The few methods that learn a value decouple it from policy
improvement itself: the value filters the dataset~\citep{grrl}, performs best-of-$N$ selection over
samples drawn from the policy~\citep{vgps, deas}, or scores commitments whose length is fixed in
advance~\citep{corft}. We argue that these limitations are structural rather than incidental, arising
from two distinct sources. Sampling-based selection is bounded by the support of what the sampler
proposes, and the induced argmax preferentially selects overestimated candidates~\citep{emaq}; a
critic over fixed whole chunks cannot distinguish skillful behavior from fortunate outcomes in
closed-loop demonstrations, and systematically overestimates commitments that succeeded by
chance~\citep{dqc}. The decisive question is therefore not only how accurately the value is learned,
but \emph{where it is permitted to act}.

We propose to let the value act on the commitment itself. The chunk is also what makes the long
horizon tractable: backing up over $h$ steps at once is the reason chunked TD remains effective in
sparse, long-horizon manipulation~\citep{qc}, and valuing every prefix of the chunk preserves this
benefit while restoring the reactivity that a fixed commitment forfeits. Human motor control offers a
natural precedent: skilled movement alternates between non-Markovian segments, executed ballistically
from an internal plan without consulting the current state, and reactive phases under feedback whose
delays render even the corrections intermittent~\citep{woodworth1899, todorov2002, gawthrop2011}.
Prefix-valued critics have appeared as RL algorithms in state-based settings~\citep{acsac, aqc}; our
contribution is to turn this construction into a post-training instrument for a pretrained VLA. A
causal-transformer critic is learned entirely offline on the VLA's frozen representations with
conservative in-sample targets~\citep{iql, deas}, and acts through two channels that remain within
the data: determining, state by state, how much of each predicted chunk to execute, and distilling
its preferences back into the policy. Freezing the backbone constrains where knowledge resides, not
the quality of the resulting behavior: the support of the demonstrations contains better behavior
than their average, which imitation blurs and extraction recovers, and commitment reshapes what is
executed rather than selecting among a sampler's proposals.

In summary, our main contributions are threefold:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We analyze why the prevailing uses of a learned value on a VLA cannot surpass the policy
    they start from, separating the two failure sources, the support ceiling of sampling-based
    selection and the skill-versus-luck miscalibration of fixed commitments, and characterizing under
    controlled conditions when each binds~\citep{emaq, dqc, park2024bottleneck}.
    \item We present a complete offline reinforcement fine-tuning pipeline in which a prefix-valued
    critic over frozen VLA representations determines state-dependent commitment and drives policy
    extraction; isolating its ingredients shows that in-sample targets and target networks are
    decisive, while low expectiles and unbounded value supports fail silently.
    \item Through experiments on VLA-scale manipulation~\citep{robocasa}, with every design choice
    isolated against its alternatives in controlled ablations, we show that the resulting policies
    surpass supervised fine-tuning using only the demonstrations it already had, without a single
    additional rollout.
\end{itemize}
Together, these findings establish offline value learning, applied where the value can genuinely act,
as a practical path to improving VLAs beyond the quality of their demonstrations.
