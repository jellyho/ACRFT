\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Vision-language-action models (VLAs) have made large-scale pretraining the dominant recipe for robot
manipulation~\citep{rt2, openvla, pi0}. Trained on large, diverse robot datasets, the vision-language
backbone provides priors strong enough that a new task rarely has to be learned from scratch, and in
practice a VLA is deployed by fine-tuning it on demonstrations of the target task.

The limitation of this recipe lies in the data it imitates. Teleoperated demonstrations are inherently
suboptimal, interleaving expert behavior with hesitation, detours, and outright failure, and their
quality varies even within a single trajectory~\citep{grrl, pistar06}. Collecting more of them does not
change this, since an imitation objective treats every transition as an equally valid target. Filtering
the data away discards exactly the scarce experience the recipe depends on, and refining the policy
through online rollouts is costly and unsafe at this scale.

Can an optimal policy be extracted from suboptimal human data with a VLA-scale model?
Temporal-difference (TD) learning and offline reinforcement learning (RL) promise precisely this,
stitching high-value segments of otherwise mediocre trajectories into behavior that no single
demonstration exhibits~\citep{cql, iql}. A VLA, however, is not the policy for which this machinery was
designed: it predicts a fixed, long action chunk~\citep{act, pi0}, it typically tackles long-horizon
tasks where standard one-step TD struggles~\citep{qc}, and its heavy backbone cannot be trained jointly
with a critic at practical cost.

Recent work has begun to bring value learning to VLAs, and its trajectory is telling. Most
post-training methods sidestep the value function altogether, letting outcome signals only condition
or reweight the imitation objective~\citep{pistar06, arfm, arm, reinbot, nora15}; they inherit the
stability of supervised learning together with its ceiling, since the policy is never trained to
exceed what it imitates. The few methods that do learn a value stop it short of the policy. GR-RL
uses its value only to filter the dataset~\citep{grrl}. V-GPS and DEAS let the value act only at test
time, rescoring chunks the policy itself proposes~\citep{vgps, deas, robomonkey}. CO-RFT trains a
chunked critic yet executes fixed-length commitments chosen in advance~\citep{corft}. We argue that
these are structural dead ends rather than accidents of engineering. A value that rescores the
policy's own proposals can never exceed what those proposals contain, and its argmax preferentially
harvests the critic's own errors~\citep{emaq}. A value over fixed whole chunks is miscalibrated by
construction: demonstrations are collected closed loop, so a chunked critic cannot distinguish skill
from luck and systematically overvalues commitments that merely happened to work~\citep{dqc}. What
decides whether offline RL helps a VLA is therefore not only how well the value is learned, but
\emph{where the value is allowed to act}.

This paper answers that question, and skilled motor behavior offers the blueprint. A human reaching
for a cup sweeps through free space ballistically, committing to a long pre-planned motion without
consulting feedback, then tightens into closed-loop control the moment contact matters; the same hand
that ignores its senses mid-swing is guided by them at the grasp~\citep{woodworth1899, todorov2002}.
Even the corrections are not continuous: sensory delays force behavior to be assembled from short
open-loop segments punctuated by intermittent replanning~\citep{gawthrop2011}. Acting well, in other
words, is not a choice between committed and reactive control but the judgment of which one each
moment demands. A VLA executing a fixed-length chunk is denied exactly this judgment, and a learned
value is exactly what can supply it. We therefore let the value act on the policy through the two
channels that stay inside the data: deciding, state by state, how much of each predicted chunk the
robot executes, and retraining the policy toward the behavior the value prefers. We instantiate this
principle as an offline reinforcement fine-tuning method for VLAs: a prefix-valued causal-transformer
critic~\citep{acsac, aqc} over the VLA's frozen representations, trained with conservative in-sample
chunk-level targets~\citep{iql, deas}, deployed for state-dependent commitment, and distilled back
into the policy. The result surpasses supervised fine-tuning from the very same demonstrations,
without a single additional rollout.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(i)~We give a structural analysis of why the prevailing ways of using a learned value on a VLA,
filtering data, rescoring samples at test time, and executing fixed commitments, cannot surpass the
policy they start from, and confirm each failure mode in controlled experiments~\citep{emaq, dqc,
park2024bottleneck}. (ii)~We propose the principle that follows: the value must act through in-data
channels, state-dependent commitment and policy extraction, and we instantiate it as a complete
offline reinforcement fine-tuning pipeline on frozen VLA representations, requiring no online
interaction. (iii)~We provide a component-isolated stability recipe for chunk-space TD at VLA scale,
identifying what is decisive and what silently fails. (iv)~We validate the pipeline end to end on
standard offline RL benchmarks~\citep{ogbench} and VLA-scale manipulation~\citep{robocasa}, using only
the demonstrations that supervised fine-tuning already uses.
