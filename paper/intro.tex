\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Vision-language-action models (VLAs) have made large-scale pretraining the dominant recipe for robot
manipulation~\citep{rt2, openvla, pi0}. The pretrained backbone provides priors strong enough that a
new task rarely starts from scratch; in practice, a VLA is deployed by fine-tuning it on
demonstrations of the target task.

The limitation of this recipe lies in the data it imitates. Teleoperated demonstrations interleave
expert behavior with hesitation, detours, and failure, and their quality varies even within a single
trajectory~\citep{grrl, pistar06}. An imitation objective treats every transition as an equally valid
target, so collecting more data reproduces the same average; filtering discards the scarce experience
the recipe depends on, and online refinement is costly and unsafe at this scale.

Can an optimal policy be extracted from suboptimal human data with a VLA-scale model?
Temporal-difference (TD) learning promises precisely this, stitching high-value segments of mediocre
trajectories into behavior no single demonstration exhibits~\citep{cql, iql}. But a VLA is not the
policy this machinery was designed for: it predicts a fixed, long action chunk~\citep{act, pi0}, it
faces long horizons where one-step TD struggles~\citep{qc}, and its backbone cannot be trained
jointly with a critic at practical cost.

Recent attempts stop short of this promise. Most reshape imitation with outcome
signals~\citep{pistar06, arfm, arm, reinbot, nora15}, inheriting both the stability and the ceiling of
supervised learning. The few that learn a value keep it away from the policy: it filters
data~\citep{grrl}, rescores the policy's own samples~\citep{vgps, deas, robomonkey}, or scores
commitments fixed in advance~\citep{corft}. We argue these are structural limits rather than
implementation accidents, with two distinct sources. Rescoring a fixed sampler is bounded by the
support of its proposals, and its argmax preferentially selects critic error~\citep{emaq}; a critic
over fixed whole chunks cannot tell skill from luck in closed-loop demonstrations, and overvalues
commitments that merely happened to work~\citep{dqc}. What matters is not only how well the value is
learned, but \emph{where it is allowed to act}.

Our answer is to let the value act on the commitment itself. The chunk is what tames the long
horizon: backing up over $h$ steps at once is precisely why chunked TD survives sparse, long-horizon
manipulation~\citep{qc}, and valuing every prefix of the chunk preserves this benefit while restoring
the reactivity that a fixed commitment forfeits. Skilled motor control offers a familiar picture of
the resulting behavior, where movement alternates between ballistic, open-loop stretches and
feedback-guided phases assembled through intermittent replanning~\citep{woodworth1899, todorov2002,
gawthrop2011}. Prefix-valued critics have appeared as RL algorithms in state-based
settings~\citep{acsac, aqc}; our contribution is to turn this construction into a post-training
instrument for a pretrained VLA. A causal-transformer critic is learned purely offline on the VLA's
frozen representations with conservative in-sample targets~\citep{iql, deas}, and acts through two
in-data channels: deciding, state by state, how much of each predicted chunk to execute, and
distilling its preferences back into the policy. Freezing the backbone constrains where knowledge
lives, not how good behavior can get: the demonstrations' support contains better behavior than their
average, which imitation blurs and extraction recovers, and commitment reshapes what gets executed
rather than rescoring a fixed sampler's proposals.

In summary, our main contributions are threefold:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We analyze why the prevailing uses of a learned value on a VLA cannot surpass the policy
    they start from, separating the two failure sources, the support ceiling of rescoring a fixed
    sampler and the skill-versus-luck miscalibration of fixed commitments, and characterizing under
    controlled conditions when each binds~\citep{emaq, dqc, park2024bottleneck}.
    \item We present a complete offline reinforcement fine-tuning pipeline in which a prefix-valued
    critic over frozen VLA representations decides state-dependent commitment and drives policy
    extraction; isolating its ingredients shows that in-sample targets and target networks are
    decisive, while low expectiles and unbounded value supports fail silently.
    \item We validate each claim on the ground that can bear it: controlled offline RL
    benchmarks~\citep{ogbench} isolate every design choice against its alternatives, and VLA-scale
    manipulation~\citep{robocasa} shows the full pipeline surpassing supervised fine-tuning using
    only the demonstrations it already had, without a single additional rollout.
\end{itemize}
Together, these findings establish offline value learning, applied where the value can genuinely act,
as a practical path to improving VLAs beyond the quality of their demonstrations.
