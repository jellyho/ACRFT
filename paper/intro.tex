\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Vision-language-action models (VLAs) have made large-scale pretraining the dominant recipe for robot
manipulation~\citep{rt2, openvla, pi0}. A pretrained backbone is adapted to a target task by
supervised fine-tuning on teleoperated demonstrations. The weakness of this recipe is the data it
imitates. Demonstrations interleave expert behavior with hesitation, detours, and failure, and their
quality varies even within a single trajectory~\citep{grrl, reinbot}; because an imitation objective
treats every transition as an equally valid target, the policy converges to the average of this
mixture. Additional data reproduces the same average more faithfully, filtering discards the scarce
experience the recipe depends on, and refining the policy through online interaction remains costly
and unsafe at this scale.

The suboptimality of demonstrations is widely recognized, and offline reinforcement learning (RL) is
its natural remedy. Because outcomes are recorded with every trajectory, a learned value can prefer
the good segments of mediocre demonstrations and stitch them into behavior that no single
demonstration exhibits~\citep{levine2020offline, fu2020d4rl}. We argue, however, that human data
poses a second problem along an axis that data quality does not capture. A teleoperator does not
emit a Markovian stream of state-conditioned actions. Skilled human control alternates between
committed, non-Markovian stretches executed from an internal plan without consulting the scene, and
reactive phases in which delayed feedback drives intermittent
corrections~\citep{woodworth1899, todorov2002, gawthrop2011}. How long to commit is therefore itself
part of the behavior the data records, and it varies from state to state. Nor is this an artifact
of imperfect learning. In the idealized limit of deterministic dynamics and full observability every
execution length performs identically, and that tie is broken precisely by what real tasks contain,
with stochastic branching rewarding frequent replanning on one side, and error re-injection under
frequent feedback together with partial observability rewarding longer commitment on the
other~\citep{zhang2025chunking, metelli2020persistence}. Offline RL for VLAs should
accordingly optimize not only which actions to take but also this second dimension, the commitment,
and adaptive action chunking is exactly the machinery for it.

Existing adaptations of RL to VLAs serve neither dimension fully. Most retain the supervised
objective and let outcome signals reshape it~\citep{pistar06, arfm, arm, reinbot, nora15}, inheriting
its ceiling. Methods that learn a value tend to apply it around the policy rather than to it,
filtering the dataset~\citep{grrl} or selecting among sampled chunks at test
time~\citep{vgps, deas}; the value then only chooses among options that already exist, and
sampling-based selection is bounded by what the sampler proposes and biased toward overestimated
candidates~\citep{emaq}.

A more direct line of work lets the value train the policy itself, yet still stops short.
Actor-critic fine-tuning over chunks exists~\citep{corft}, but treats
the chunk as a fixed, indivisible action, even though a critic scoring whole chunks of closed-loop
demonstrations conflates skill with fortunate outcomes~\citep{dqc}. Adaptive chunking itself has been
developed within RL~\citep{qc, aqc, acsac}; chunk-level TD makes long horizons tractable and prefix
values let the commitment vary. Yet the commitment is still selected by scoring candidates at
deployment rather than optimized jointly with the actions. Selection alone also cannot shrink the
share of the commitment trade-off that stems from policy error, which only updating the policy
removes, and recent execution-length methods indeed resort to attaching a separate
policy-improvement stage to move past the frozen action distribution~\citep{exrl}. Moreover, no
such construction has been developed into a purely offline post-training stage that leaves a VLA
better than its supervised starting point. What is missing is a method that
optimizes both dimensions at once, directly on the policy, at VLA scale.

We present such a method, an offline reinforcement fine-tuning stage that improves the policy over
the joint space of what to do and how long to commit. A causal-transformer critic valuing every
prefix of the action chunk is learned entirely offline on the VLA's frozen representations with
conservative in-sample targets~\citep{iql, deas}, and the policy is updated against it in both
dimensions of its output; the actions within the chunk are pulled toward what the value prefers, and
the commitment length becomes a state-dependent decision rather than a constant. Nothing is sampled
and ranked at deployment. Freezing the backbone constrains where knowledge resides, not the quality
of the resulting behavior; the support of the demonstrations contains better behavior than their
average, which imitation blurs and extraction recovers.

In summary, our main contributions are threefold:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We analyze why the prevailing mechanisms for applying a learned value to a VLA,
    sampling-based selection and fixed-length execution, cannot surpass the policy they start from,
    and locate the missing piece in direct policy optimization over both actions and
    commitments~\citep{emaq, dqc, park2024bottleneck}.
    \item We present a complete offline reinforcement fine-tuning method for VLAs that jointly
    optimizes the policy over actions and commitment lengths through a single learned value,
    together with a training recipe that keeps chunk-level value learning stable at this scale,
    identifying which components are essential and which fail silently.
    \item Through experiments on challenging manipulation tasks in both simulation~\citep{robocasa}
    and the real world, with every design choice isolated against its alternatives in controlled
    ablations, we show that the resulting policies surpass supervised fine-tuning using only the
    demonstrations it already had, without a single additional rollout.
\end{itemize}
Together, these findings establish offline value learning, applied where the value can genuinely act,
as a practical path to improving VLAs beyond the quality of their demonstrations.
